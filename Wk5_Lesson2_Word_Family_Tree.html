Case Notes

Six clues for this week’s trickiest spellings. Say each word aloud as you read.

1 · -ent goes with -ence

When a describing word ends in -ent, its noun ends in -ence: independent → independence, confident → confidence, resilient → resilience. The e stays in the family.

Remember: -ent → -ence. The e stays.

2 · -ant goes with -ance

It works the other way too. When a word ends in -ant, its noun ends in -ance: important → importance, distant → distance, tolerant → tolerance.

Remember: -ant → -ance. The a stays.

3 · Why you can’t hear it: the schwa

Say CON-fi-dence. The first beat is loud; the ending is quiet and squashed into an “uh” sound called the schwa. -ence and -ance sound the same, so your ears can’t tell you. Use the family word instead.

Remember: Can’t hear the vowel? Think of a family member you already know.

4 · responsible → responsibility

Words ending in -ible make nouns ending in -ibility: the le changes to ility and the i stays: responsible → responsibility, possible → possibility. Opportunity uses plain -ity too, with a double p.

Remember: -ible → -ibility. Keep the i.

5 · The xi in anxious

Say ANK-shus. The “sh” sound is spelt xi — a rare spelling! The family word anxiety reminds you there’s an x. And it ends in -ous, like nervous.

Remember: anxious = an + xi + ous. The x is really there.

6 · The sch in schedule

Some people say “sked-ule” (ch = /k/, like school and chemist) and some say “shed-ule”. Either way, the spelling starts with sch and ends with -ule, like module.

Remember: A schedule is for school — both start with sch.
