Case Notes

Six clues for cracking this week’s trickiest spellings. Say each word slowly and listen.

1 · The silent p in receipt

We don’t say the p in receipt, but we write it. Its family word reception (a place where things are received) lets you hear it. Also: after c, it’s ei.

Remember: Think “reception” to remember the p in receipt.

2 · The silent u in guarantee

The u in guarantee is silent. The gu team keeps the g sound hard, just like in guard, guess and guide. And it ends with a double ee.

Remember: g-u “guards” the hard g. Double e at the end.

3 · persuade → persuasive

The verb is persuade. To make the describing word, the de changes to s and we add -ive: persuasive. The same swap makes persuasion. (Same pattern: decide → decisive, invade → invasion.)

Remember: persuade loses its de and gains an s: persuasive.

4 · Keep the e, or drop the e?

When a suffix starts with a consonant (like -ment), keep the silent e: advertisement. When it starts with a vowel (like -er), drop the e: consumer.

Remember: Consonant suffix — keep the e. Vowel suffix — drop the e.

5 · One c, double s in necessity

In necessity you hear two /s/ sounds, but they’re spelt differently: a soft c (c before e says /s/) and then a double s. Try: “one Collar, two Sleeves”.

Remember: necessity has one collar (c) and two sleeves (ss).

6 · Endings that hide: -ase and -ence

Say PUR-chase and IN-flu-ence. The stress is at the start, so the endings go soft and blurry. You can’t trust your ears here — learn them: purchase, influence.

Remember: Unstressed endings are blurry. purchase ends -ase; influence ends -ence.
