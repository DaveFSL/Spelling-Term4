Case Notes

Six rules for building this week’s science words from their parts.

1 · ir- means “not” (before r)

The prefix in- means “not”. Before a word starting with r, it changes to ir-. That’s why irreversible has a double r: ir + reversible.

Remember: ir + reversible = irreversible.

2 · dis- + solve = double s

Dis- can mean “apart”. When sugar dissolves, it spreads out through the water. The s of dis meets the s of solve, so there are two.

Remember: dis + solve = dissolve.

3 · -ible or -able?

-able usually follows a whole word (wash → washable). -ible usually follows a part that isn’t a word by itself (visible, possible). Reversible comes from Latin and takes -ible — learn it as a word to watch.

Remember: reversible — with an i.

4 · -ation names a process

-ation turns an action into the name of a process. Evaporate → evaporation. Condense → condensation (drop the e, keep the s, add -ation).

Remember: -ation names the process: condens + ation.

5 · ch says /k/, ph says /f/

Science words often come from Greek. In chemical, ch says /k/. In physical, ph says /f/ — from the Greek physis, meaning “nature”.

Remember: Hear /k/ in chemical? Write ch. Hear /f/ in physical? Write ph.

6 · -ture says “cher”

At the end of a word, the sound “cher” is often spelt -ture: mix + ture = mixture, just like picture and nature.

Remember: mixture ends like picture: -ture.
