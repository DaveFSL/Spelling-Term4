Case Notes

This week’s eight words come in four pairs. Read each card — you’ll need them to sort the evidence.

1 · Reversible and irreversible

A reversible change can be undone — the material can go back to how it was. An irreversible change can’t be undone.

Remember: Reversible = it can go back. Irreversible = it can never go back.

2 · Physical and chemical changes

A physical change alters a material’s shape or state, but it’s still the same substance. A chemical change makes a brand-new substance — like rust or ash.

Remember: Chemical change = a new substance is made.

3 · Dissolve and mixture

When salt or sugar dissolves, it spreads through the water so you can’t see it — but it’s still there. A mixture is two or more materials mixed together that can be separated again.

Remember: A dissolved solid is still there — you can get it back.

4 · Evaporation and condensation

Evaporation is when a liquid turns into a gas (water vapour). Condensation is when that gas cools and turns back into liquid drops — like on a cold glass.

Remember: Evaporation: liquid to gas. Condensation: gas back to liquid.

Explain One

Pick one irreversible change you sorted. Write two sentences explaining why it can’t go back. Use at least two focus words.

Sentence starters

• When … , it is an irreversible change because …
• It is a chemical change, because a new substance …
• You can’t get the … back, unlike …

Tap a word to drop it into your writing box.

🔒 Write at least one sentence of your own first — then the worked example unlocks.
Worked example (one way to do it): Rusting is an irreversible change because the iron nail turns into rust. It is a chemical change, since rust is a new substance and you can never turn it back into shiny iron. It names the change, says why it can’t go back, and uses two focus words.

Detective challenge (extension)

Add a sentence using an extension word: combustion (burning), corrosion (a metal slowly being eaten away, like rust) or solubility (how well something dissolves).
