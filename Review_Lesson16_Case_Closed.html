Case Summary

One card per case file. Each one is a strategy you can use on words you’ve never seen before.

Case File 1 · Poetry: word origins

Odd spelling? Ask where the word came from. Greek words like rhyme and physical use rh, ph and y. Borrowed words keep their home-country spelling.

Remember: Weird letters often mean a word from another language.

Case File 2 · Catholic Mission: noun suffixes

The suffixes -ity and -ion turn words into nouns: equal → equality, act → action. Watch the -ous change, as in curious → curiosity: the u drops out.

Remember: -ous → -osity. -ity and -ion make naming words.

Case File 3 · Science: prefixes and suffixes

A prefix sits in front and keeps every letter — so two letters can meet: ir + regular, mis + spell. Suffixes like -ation build nouns: inform → information.

Remember: Prefix + word = keep all the letters, even when they double.

Case File 4 · HASS: hidden letters

Some letters are written but hidden. A family word often shows them: design → designate, debt → debit. Word changes matter too: decide → decisive (de becomes s).

Remember: Look for a family word that shows the hidden letter.

Case File 5 · HPE: word families

Not sure whether the ending is -ence or -ance? Think of a family member you already know: confident → confidence, possible → possibility. Families keep their letters.

Remember: Not sure of an ending? Ask the word’s family.

    Case Closed

    Last task, detective: explain the strategy you used for two tricky words. Then tick off your evidence.

    Strategy prompts

    • Word origins: For … , I remembered it comes from … , so it uses …
    • Suffixes: I knew … ends in … , so the noun is …
    • Prefixes: I added … to … and kept all the letters.
    • Hidden letters: I thought of the family word … to remember the …
    • Word families: Because … ends in -ent, the noun ends in …

    🔒 Write your own explanation first — then the worked example unlocks.
