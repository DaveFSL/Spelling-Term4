Case Notes

Six rules for building mission words. Suffixes change a word’s job — and sometimes its spelling.

1 · -ity makes a naming word

Adding -ity turns a describing word into a naming word: equal → equality. Charity, dignity, community and solidarity all end in -ity. They are naming words.

Remember: -ity makes a naming word: equal → equality.

2 · -ous → -osity: the u drops out

When an -ous word takes -ity, the u drops out: generous → generosity, curious → curiosity. Listen: the beat moves to OS, so you can clearly hear the o.

Remember: Generosity has no u. Say gen-er-OS-i-ty.

3 · -ion names an action (drop the e)

-ion turns an action into a naming word. If the action ends in e, drop it first: donate → donation, celebrate → celebration.

Remember: -ion names the action. Drop a final e first.

4 · com- means “with” or “together”

The Latin com- means “with”. Compassion literally means “suffering with” someone. com- meets a root that starts with m, so community has a double m.

Remember: com- = with. com- meets a root that starts with m, so community has a double m.

5 · -eer means “a person who”

The ending -eer names a person who does something. A volunteer is a person who offers to help. The “ear” sound at the end is spelt eer.

Remember: volunteer ends in -eer, like engineer.

6 · The -ice ending in justice

At the end of justice, the /is/ sound is spelt -ice — the same as notice and service. It is not “justis” or “justise”.

Remember: justice is just + ice.
