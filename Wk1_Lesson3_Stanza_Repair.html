Case Notes

Two tools for proofreading like a detective.

1 · Proofread slowly

✗ rythm → ✓ rhythm

Your brain reads what it expects to see. Slow down, point at one word at a time, and ask: does this look right?

Remember: Read every word, not the word you expect.

2 · Know each word’s trap

Each word has one spot where people slip. If you know the trap, you can check it on purpose.

Remember: Check the orange part of each word first.

My Verse

Write a two-line verse of your own. Use one poet’s tool (a simile, metaphor or alliteration) and at least two focus words. The Spelling Spy lights up each focus word you spell correctly.

Line starters

• The creek ran like … (simile)
• The night was a … (metaphor)
• Six sleepy sheep … (alliteration)

Try a focus word: “…with the rhythm of the rain”, “…the last stanza of the day”, “…a ballad for the bush”.

Tap a word to drop it into your writing box.

🔒 Write at least one line of your own first — then the worked example unlocks.
Worked example (one way to do it): The creek was a ribbon of silver light,
its rhythm the heartbeat of our ballad tonight. Tool: a metaphor (the creek was a ribbon). Focus words: rhythm, ballad. The lines rhyme too (light / tonight).
